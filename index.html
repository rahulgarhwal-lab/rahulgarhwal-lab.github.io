Questions behind the Lab

The hardest questions begin after the capability exists.

Technology creates possibility. Value depends on where it is applied, how it enters the work, and whether the organization can prove and scale the result.

Choose where to intervene → Make intelligence usable → Prove and scale value
01 · Intervention

Where should capability change the business?

Not every process needs more intelligence, automation, or AI. The first choice is where better support can materially change growth, cost, risk, customer experience, or execution.

Which decisions are consequential enough to redesign?

Who makes the decision today? What makes it difficult? What information is missing? What should improve if the decision changes?

What should AI support, automate, or leave human?

Consequence, uncertainty, context, reversibility, and accountability should determine where AI assists and where judgment remains human.

02 · Use

What makes intelligence usable in practice?

Access to information does not guarantee attention, trust, action, or adoption. Intelligence must become relevant in the moment and environment where the work occurs.

When does more intelligence create less focus?

Signals, predictions, alerts, and recommendations can increase cognitive burden when users must determine what matters on their own.

Why do capable products fail to become part of the work?

Workflow fit, timing, trust, ownership, and a clear next action determine whether a capability changes behavior or remains another destination.

03 · Value

What turns product success into enterprise value?

A product may achieve adoption and measurable results within one boundary while the wider business capability remains fragmented.

What must change beyond the product boundary?

Ownership, decision rights, incentives, governance, and operating-model alignment often determine whether success can extend across the enterprise.

What evidence proves value was realized?

Delivery, access, and usage show activity. Changed decisions, behavior, efficiency, risk, engagement, and economic outcomes show whether something important improved.

Working point of view

Capability is only the beginning.

Enterprise discussions often jump from the capability being built to the outcome being promised. The connections between them determine whether value is realized.

Current explorations

Working ideas shaped through practice.

These are not finished doctrines. They reflect patterns I have encountered, contributed to, sometimes misunderstood, and continued to refine.

Exploration 01

AI accelerates the path. It does not define the destination.

I have been as susceptible as anyone to beginning with what a new technology makes possible. Experience keeps bringing me back to a more basic question: what commercial decision, action, or outcome are we actually trying to improve?

Common failure pattern

Starting with an interesting capability before defining the commercial decision.

Difficult trade-off

Automation speed versus account context, relationship risk, and accountability.

Diagnostic question

Would the problem still matter if AI were removed from the proposal?

AI should compress the distance between a meaningful commercial signal and an accountable action—without removing the judgment required to understand the customer, context, and consequence.

Exploration 02

When more intelligence creates more complexity.

Additional information can improve decisions. It can also transfer the burden of interpretation from the organization to the user. The product becomes comprehensive while the user becomes overwhelmed.

Common failure pattern

Generating more signals without deciding what deserves attention now.

Difficult trade-off

Broad coverage versus relevance, focus, and explainable prioritization.

Diagnostic question

Are we helping the user decide—or simply organizing more information?

Adaptive Intelligence asks what should change according to the user, objective, situation, available context, and decision being made.

Exploration 03

From proven products to enterprise transformation.

A product can have sustained users, measurable value, executive support, and a credible roadmap while the broader business problem remains fragmented across systems, functions, ownership boundaries, and investment decisions.

Common failure pattern

Attempting to scale transformation by scaling the product alone.

Difficult trade-off

Enterprise consistency versus local relevance and distributed expertise.

Diagnostic question

Is the organization scaling software—or changing how the enterprise operates?

Product scale and enterprise transformation are related, but they are not the same organizational achievement.

Current Exploration 04

From individual assistants to one enterprise intelligence interface.

Product-level assistants are making enterprise tools easier to use. As these capabilities multiply, I am exploring whether one role-aware conversational interface can connect governed products, data, and workflows into one coherent enterprise experience—carrying context from question to decision to action.

Status Active product and architecture exploration; not yet a proven enterprise capability.

Common pattern

Useful assistants improve individual products, while users still move between tools and carry context across systems themselves.

Difficult trade-off

A shared interface must reduce navigation burden without weakening permissions, source authority, specialized context, or human accountability.

Diagnostic question

Are individual assistants reducing the user’s total effort across the journey—or only improving isolated steps within separate products?

What is being explored: a role-aware interface connected to governed enterprise intelligence and workflows through MCP-enabled architecture, starting with one controlled use case to test security, context, user experience, and business value.

Why I see the problem this way

One question, viewed through an expanding field of responsibility.

The roles, tools, and scale changed. The recurring problem did not: how to connect information to a consequential decision and make the result usable in practice.

The recurring question

The information existed. The connection to the decision did not.

My path into digital products began with that tension. Each stage added a new dimension: understanding the system, structuring intelligence, making it repeatable, placing it in the workflow, owning the portfolio, and increasing its scale with AI.

Science

Understand systems, evidence, dependencies, and cause and effect.

Commercial intelligence

Structure customer, product, market, and scientific relationships.

Digital products

Make intelligence repeatable, accessible, governed, and scalable.

Workflow integration

Place intelligence where people plan, decide, and act.

Portfolio responsibility

Connect strategy, trade-offs, delivery, adoption, and measurable value.

Applied AI

Increase the scale and speed of interpretation while preserving judgment.

Today: I apply this perspective across a global portfolio of commercial analytics, data, and digital products.
